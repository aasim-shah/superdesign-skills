# Inspiration: reference designs, broken down

Eight compositions clients pointed to as "this is the level", described so the patterns can be reused without copying anyone's design, plus one page made with this skill that a client approved (`examples/walkaway-home/`, with source and screenshots). Read with `creative-direction.md`. Take the structure, never the brand, the copy or the imagery, and never the example's look for another product.

## 1. Pick a pattern by product

| The product is | Lead with | Section |
|---|---|---|
| A physical object | The object placed in a real scene, glass over the photograph | 2, 3 |
| A phone app | Real screenshots in a device frame cut out of a panel, callouts on the UI | 3, 9, and `examples/walkaway-home/` |
| A service people use in the world (rides, rentals, places) | A bento of photographs and a map tile with notched joins | 4 |
| A platform or B2B service with nothing to photograph | One abstract hero object (a globe, a network) with label pills and glass notes | 6 |
| A web app or dashboard | A two-tone shell, big numbers, one chart color | 7 |
| Plans or products to compare | Notched cards with the action button nested in the corner | 5 |
| One iconic object (an instrument, a bottle, a shoe, a device) | Two color fields with the object standing across the seam | 8 |
| A warm, friendly or playful brand (education, food, kids, community) | Bands joined by waves or blobs, a photo or video card riding on a band | 9 |

## 2. Atmospheric product scene

A smart-ring concept. One full-bleed photograph carries the whole hero.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The scene | A rounded panel filled edge to edge by one photograph: a twilight sky fading from violet to peach, soft mountains, a mossy rock with flowers in the foreground. One atmosphere, many hues, all from the photograph |
| The product | The ring, large, sitting on the rock as if it belongs there, lit by the same sky; the brightest, sharpest object in the frame |
| Headline | Large, light grotesk, white, three lines on the left; the last word tinted in the scene's accent as a gradient. A short rule, then one sentence |
| Action | One pill button with the arrow in its own circle at the right end; a single quiet pill in the header |
| Glass over the scene | Translucent cards on the photograph, never on flat color: three stacked feature pills (icon in a soft circle, title, one-line sub), a "Powered by" card with a 3D chip, a portrait photo card with an arrow button |
| A second statement | Lower right, another large headline straight on the photograph with two words tinted, and a small icon with a two-line sub |
| Navigation | Mark and wordmark, three centered text links, one pill: very little chrome |

Why it reads as premium: one coherent light source and palette from a real scene; the product integrated into its environment; every UI element is glass over the scene; large light white type, color only as tints taken from the scene.

## 3. Product cutout with callouts

A plant-shop concept. A product cutout on a flat dark panel.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The panel | A deep green rounded panel inset in a white page |
| The product | A real photo cutout, center right, with out-of-focus leaves floating around it for depth |
| Callouts | Four glass notes with an icon, each joined by a leader line to a dot on the product, with real facts ("Water level: 450ml") |
| Copy | Product name large on the left, price and social proof, one paragraph, quantity stepper, one button, a "Know more" link |
| Cards | A row of product cards below, each with its cutout breaking out over the card's top edge; carousel arrows; the selected card tinted |

Why it works: the product is real and annotated with real facts; it breaks the frame it sits in; depth comes from blur and overlap.

## 4. Bento photo collage with notched joins

A bike-share app hero. Light and calm, the photographs do the selling.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The panel | A very light blue-white panel with a large radius (about 48px) on a softly textured grey page |
| Copy | Bold geometric sans headline in three lines, left; one sentence; a solid primary pill with a download arrow and an outline secondary pill |
| The bento | Right half: three tiles of different sizes (a map with bike-count pins, a street photograph, a close photograph of the docked bikes) packed together; where tiles meet, the corners are concave, so each tile seems carved from one shape |
| Icon row | Three icon buttons in soft circles (scan, bike, parking) nested into the space the bento leaves at its lower right |

Why it works: real-world photographs and a real map tile prove the service exists; the notched joins make several images read as one designed object instead of a grid of boxes; one accent (blue) for the action, pins and icons.

## 5. Notched pricing cards

The same bike-share app's plan cards.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The card | Rounded card (about 40px) with a concave bite cut out of its lower right corner |
| The action | A round arrow button sits in the bite, separated from the card by a thin gap, as if the button carved it |
| Content | Plan name in a bold sans, one sentence, the product photo in an inner rounded tile, the price in a pill at the lower left |
| Selection | The recommended card is tinted (light blue) and its arrow button is solid accent; the others are white with outline buttons |

Why it works: the notch turns a plain card into a shaped object and points the eye at the action; the same product photo in every card keeps the comparison honest.

## 6. Abstract hero object with label pills

A hiring-platform concept. Nothing physical to show, so the hero is one symbolic object.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The frame | The whole hero sits in a rounded panel with a thin white border, inside a slightly lighter version of the same color |
| The scene | A warm monochrome photograph in burnt orange: two hands reaching towards each other from the edges |
| The object | A white wireframe globe (a point-and-line mesh) between the hands, with curved arcs connecting white country pills, each with its flag |
| Copy | A large, centered, white sans headline over two lines, one centered sentence, one light pill button |
| Glass notes | Two translucent cards left and right of the globe, a title and three lines each, overlapping its edge |
| Navigation | A segmented pill nav centered with the active item filled; one outline pill on the right |

Why it works: one hue in many tones (a monochrome photograph) reads rich without being colorful; the wireframe object and pills make an abstract service concrete; centered symmetric composition gives calm.

## 7. Two-tone app shell

A hiring dashboard. Restraint is the design.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The shell | A deep green sidebar and a warm off-white content area, both inside one rounded frame; the active nav item is a white pill |
| Numbers | Stat cards lead with a big light number, a small green delta and "vs last month", with one mini chart each (bars with one dark bar, a sparkline with a soft fill, avatar stacks) |
| Charts | Neutral greys plus the one dark green; a second and third color appear only in the pipeline bar, where they mean something |
| Detail | A headline number with its unit in grey ("3,480 Candidates"), segmented tabs, dashed dividers, a quiet table with status chips |
| Chrome | Hairline borders, generous padding, one dark primary button |

Why it works: two surfaces and one brand color; type size, not color, sets the hierarchy; charts are small and readable.

## 8. Split color field with the object on the seam

A guitar shop hero. Two flat colors and one object do everything.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The fields | The hero is split vertically: a pale cream field on the left (about 55%), a deep brown field on the right; both inside a frame of a third, mid-brown tone |
| The object | A real guitar photo cutout stands exactly on the seam: its body sits half on each field, its headstock rises past the top of the frame. It joins the two colors, so the split reads as one composition |
| Echo shapes | Two soft circles in tones of the two fields sit behind the guitar's body, one on each side, extending the object into the fields |
| Copy | On the light field only: a hand-lettered, underlined display headline, one bold sentence, one dark pill button; nav links in the light field, the cart pill and social icons in the dark field |

Why it works: maximum contrast with only two colors; the object is the bridge, so the eye lands on it first; each field has one job (the light one talks, the dark one frames).

Build it with `Split` (`components/Sections.tsx`): `left`, `right`, `at` (the seam, in percent) and `slant` (px; 0 is straight). Put the object at `left: {at}%` with `-translate-x-1/2` and `data-sig-seam`, and let it break the top or bottom of the section. With a slant, the seam's top is at `at% + slant/2` and its bottom at `at% - slant/2`.

## 9. Shaped section edges

A school site. Sections do not end in straight lines.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| The band | A saturated mustard band on a pale cream page; its top and bottom edges are organic waves, and a second wave lobe rises from below into it |
| Content on the band | A heading and a paragraph on the left; a video card (photo with a round play button) on the right, its caption in white on the band |
| Rhythm | The next section returns to cream with a centered heading and a short accent underline |

Why it works: the shaped edges make the page feel made by hand and friendly; a single saturated band between calm sections gives a clear beat.

Build edges with `Edge` (`components/Sections.tsx`): `shape` is `tilt`, `zigzag`, `wave`, `curve` or `blob`; `fill` is the neighbor's color as a fill class; `at="bottom"` rises from the bottom of a section into it, `at="top"` hangs from the top; `flip` mirrors it; `teeth` sets the zigzag count. The parent section needs `relative` and enough padding for the edge's height. Pick one edge family per site: waves and blobs for warm brands, tilts for confident or technical ones, zigzags for playful ones or a single accent moment. `data-edge` (set by the component) makes the shape swell into place on scroll.

## 10. What they share

- The product (or a symbol of it) is the largest, sharpest thing on screen, and real wherever possible.
- One dominant atmosphere: a photograph, or one panel color. Accents are tints taken from it, or one brand color.
- Shapes are designed, not default: panels inset with large radii, notched corners, tiles carved from one shape, edges the product breaks.
- Glass and pills are laid over the image or panel, not boxes on flat paper.
- Large, confident headlines (light or bold, never medium), one sentence, one primary action, one secondary.
- Little chrome: a short nav, often a single pill or segmented control.

## 11. Applying them without photography

- **App products**: real screenshots in a phone frame are the product (`components/Cutouts.tsx`), on one panel color with a halo, annotated with `Callout` and `Leads`.
- **A scene or a bento**: ask the client for photography first. Stock is a fallback only with the user's yes: a free-license photo (for example Unsplash) is downloaded, uploaded with `upload.py` and shown to the user before use. Never hotlink, never generate images with Superdesign credits.
- **A symbolic object**: build it in SVG (a point-and-line globe, a network, arcs between pills) and animate it with DrawSVG and a slow rotation.
- **Objects**: shaded SVG cutouts (gradient stops by class, `useId()` ids), one slightly blurred.
- **Notches**: `NotchCard` in `components/Cutouts.tsx` cuts the concave corner with a CSS mask and seats the round button in it.
- **The glass**: `bg-white/10` to `/60` with `backdrop-blur`, a 1px white border at 15 to 70%, over the panel or photograph.
- **A split field**: works with a shaded SVG object or a product screenshot in a frame as well as with a photo cutout; the object must cross the seam.
